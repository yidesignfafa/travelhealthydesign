THE CHALLENGE

A Systemic Disconnect

The Context: During the COVID-19 crisis, researchers and policymakers struggled to track variants across borders, leading to over $16 Trillion in global economic losses. In response, the CDC funded this initiative to modernize travel health surveillance, replacing reactive measures with proactive intelligence.

The Core Problem: Patients, clinics, and researchers operated in disconnected silos relying on manual paper based workflows. This lack of integration meant data was cost high, and incomplete, inconsistent, delayed, which could cause clinical erros, prevent real-time monitoring and coordinated response.

Part 1: The Input Engine

Solving Friction with
"Minimum Viable Data"

Designed the mobile app to remove barriers for patients.

The Problem: 83% Drop-off Rate during signup. A user testing found that asking for full medical history upfront (Consents, Travel Details, History) was too heavy before trust was established.

The Pivot: I facilitated a workshop to negotiate a "Minimum Viable Data" strategy. We cut initial fields by 30%, deferring other no critical clinical questions and setup later and collect during different travel stages when necessary.

The Core Loop:
The 60-Second Survey

The Challenge: How do we get daily medical data from travelers on vacation, busy traveling schedules or lose internet?

Dynamic Logic: If a user is healthy, the survey takes just 2 taps (30 seconds). If a user is unhealthy, the self-report survey takes 1min to finish.
Offline Resilience: The app handles synchronization silently and auto save locally and upload data when has internet access, ensuring zero data loss even in flight.

Solving Retention:
Value Exchange

To solve the "Survey Fatigue" issue, we had to shift from "Extracting Data" to "Providing Value."

Emotional (Gamification): I designed the 'Digital Postcard' system. It turned data entry into a collectible game.

Clinical Utility (Useful & Reliable): Created a Visual Health Diary (the Calendar) that travelers use to share history with their doctors and post user feedback showed users rated this feature very useful. We also have carefully provided other useful digtial health tools to help travelers stay informed and organized to increase the app retention.

Solving Complexity for
Travel Medicine

The Challenge: Malaria prevention involves 7 different drug types with overlapping schedules and easiy casue medication errors.

The Solution: I collaborated with clinicians to map the logic, shifting the burden from the User to the System. The app now automates the entire schedule based on a single input.

Part 2: The Output Engine

Visualizing Risk for
Rapid Response

For researchers, value meant insight, not just collection.

The Conflict: Stakeholders were drowning in noise. Policymakers needed macro trends to allocate funding, while epidemiologists needed granular, row-level data for contact tracing. The dashboard had to serve both.

The Solution: I designed the Risk-Density Visualization Method.
Bubble Size = Confidence and Color = Severity
This allowed an reseracher or public health agent to scan the entire situation or trend in seconds.

More Stories

To meet strict MGH privacy standards and support travelers with spotty internet, collaborating with engineers, we designed for a Local-First Architecture.
